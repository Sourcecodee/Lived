# Product 1 --- Human Experience Discovery Platform

> **Working concept:** A premium digital destination for discovering
> authentic human-created work, stories, perspectives and experiences in
> a world increasingly saturated with AI-generated content.
>
> **Working positioning:** *In a world where everything can be
> generated, experience something real.*

**Status:** Early product concept and proposed starting architecture.
This is a planning document, not a final specification. Product name,
business model and detailed feature priorities remain open.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Executive summary

The product is a discovery platform built around the value of human
creativity and experience.

AI is here to stay. The product is not designed to ban AI, attack AI
tools or insist that AI-assisted work is inherently bad. Its purpose is
to give people a place where they can intentionally discover work and
experiences that are genuinely rooted in human creativity, perspective,
effort or lived experience.

As AI makes digital content easier and cheaper to produce, the internet
may become increasingly crowded with synthetic, repetitive and
low-effort material. This platform responds by making human-origin work
easier to discover, appreciate and support.

The ambition is not simply to create another social network. It is to
create a **premium discovery experience** in which the work, the creator
and the story behind the work receive attention.

The iPhone analogy is about product philosophy and perceived experience:
thoughtful design, consistency, quality, identity and a feeling that the
product is worth returning to. It does not mean copying Apple's
interface or pricing.

## 2. The problem

Digital platforms are increasingly filled with large volumes of content.
AI tools make it possible to generate text, images, music, video and
other material at scale.

This creates several problems:

-   Human-created work can become harder to find among huge volumes of
    generated content.
-   Discovery systems often reward volume, frequency and engagement
    rather than originality or depth.
-   Independent creators can struggle to get meaningful visibility.
-   People who want human perspectives, personal stories or original
    creative work may have difficulty finding them.
-   The person behind a piece of work can become secondary to the
    content feed.
-   Users can experience content overload: more material is available,
    but finding something genuinely worthwhile takes effort.

The product should address **discovery and experience**, not attempt to
solve every problem associated with AI.

## 3. Product vision and positioning

### Vision

Become a trusted, desirable destination for discovering human creativity
and experience.

### Core promise

Help people discover things made, expressed or experienced by humans,
without having to fight through an overwhelming stream of
undifferentiated content.

### Positioning

This is a **human-origin discovery platform**, not an anti-AI platform
and not merely a portfolio site.

The product should make human work feel:

-   Valuable rather than disposable.
-   Discoverable rather than buried.
-   Personal rather than anonymous.
-   Beautifully presented rather than crowded into a generic feed.
-   Worth supporting rather than merely consuming.

### Product principles

1.  **Human-first, not anti-AI.** AI-assisted work can be allowed when
    its role is transparently disclosed.
2.  **Discovery over endless scrolling.** Help users find worthwhile
    work rather than maximise time spent in the app.
3.  **Quality of experience.** Presentation, navigation, typography,
    media and editorial curation should feel deliberate.
4.  **Creator dignity.** Give creators identity, attribution, context
    and control over their work.
5.  **Transparency.** Do not claim that a piece is verified human-made
    unless there is a defensible basis for that claim.
6.  **Trust without impossible promises.** Authenticity signals should
    communicate what is known and how it is known.
7.  **Human stories matter.** Show the person and process behind work
    when the creator chooses to share them.

## 5. Target users

### Primary audience

-   People who enjoy discovering original creative work.
-   People tired of noisy, repetitive or heavily automated content
    feeds.
-   Independent artists, photographers, writers, musicians and makers.
-   Curious users who value personal stories and distinctive
    perspectives.

### Secondary audience

-   Collectors and buyers of original creative work.
-   Creative communities and independent publishers.
-   Organisations looking to discover human creative talent.
-   Brands or businesses seeking authentic creators and collaborations.

### Initial market

The product can be designed for a global audience from the beginning,
while the initial creator community can be built through accessible
communities and networks. Avoid assuming that a global launch requires
serving every country, language and content format on day one.

## 6. The intended user experience

The first impression should feel more like entering a thoughtfully
curated digital gallery or premium independent magazine than opening a
conventional social feed.

A possible user journey:

1.  A visitor opens the platform and immediately sees a carefully
    selected collection of human-origin work.
2.  They explore by category, mood, topic or creator.
3.  They open a piece and see the work presented beautifully, with
    optional context about the creator and creation process.
4.  They can follow the creator, save the work or explore related
    creations.
5.  They can create an account to build collections and personalise
    discovery.
6.  A creator can publish work, provide context and select transparent
    origin labels.

### Potential main navigation

-   **Discover** --- curated home and new discoveries.
-   **Explore** --- categories, themes and search.
-   **Creators** --- discover people and their work.
-   **Collections** --- saved and editorial collections.
-   **Profile** --- user or creator profile.

Keep navigation simpler in the MVP. Some sections can initially be
filters or pages rather than separate complex systems.

## 7. Differentiation

The platform should not compete by trying to have the most posts, the
most features or the most addictive feed.

Its differentiation should come from:

-   A distinctive premium visual and interaction experience.
-   Human-origin content as the organising principle.
-   Strong creator presentation and attribution.
-   Intentional curation and discovery.
-   Transparent disclosure of AI involvement.
-   A culture that values originality, effort, perspective and lived
    experience.

### Relationship to existing platforms

The product may borrow useful interaction patterns from social networks,
visual discovery platforms and digital magazines, but it needs a
distinct reason to exist.

It should not become just: - Another infinite-scroll social feed. - A
generic image-sharing site. - A portfolio directory with no discovery. -
An anti-AI campaign site. - A marketplace before there is a strong
creator and audience community.

## 8. Authenticity and AI disclosure

Authenticity is central to the product, but it is also the hardest trust
problem.

A creator ticking a box that says "human-made" is not proof. The
platform must avoid presenting unverified claims as facts.

### Suggested origin labels

Use clear, non-judgemental labels such as:

-   **Human-created** --- the creator declares that the core work was
    created by a human without generative AI contributing to the work.
-   **AI-assisted** --- AI was used as part of the creative process,
    while the human contributed meaningful direction, editing or
    creation.
-   **AI-generated** --- generative AI produced the core submitted work.
-   **Origin not verified** --- the platform does not have enough
    information to make a stronger statement.

Definitions must be precise and visible. For example, ordinary editing
tools, spellcheck, image adjustments or accessibility tools should not
automatically make a work "AI-generated". The platform should define
what counts as material generative AI involvement.

### Evidence options

Creators may optionally provide: - Drafts, sketches or earlier
versions. - Process photographs or recordings. - Behind-the-scenes
material. - A short explanation of how the work was made. - Links to an
established portfolio or creator history. - Live demonstrations or other
appropriate evidence.

Evidence should be proportionate to the type of work. Do not demand
intrusive proof for every ordinary post.

### Trust model

Start with **creator declarations and transparent labels**, not a
promise of perfect detection.

Later, introduce carefully scoped verification for selected categories
or creators. Explain exactly what was checked. A verification badge
should never imply more than the verification process supports.

AI detectors should not be treated as definitive proof. They can make
mistakes and should not be the sole basis for rejecting or labelling
work.

## 9. MVP: what to build first

The first version should prove one central hypothesis:

> People want a more considered place to discover human-origin creative
> work, and creators are willing to publish there because their work
> receives better presentation and discovery.

### MVP user features

1.  Public discovery homepage with a curated selection of work.
2.  Browse a small number of content categories.
3.  Open a content detail page.
4.  View creator profiles.
5.  Search or filter by category, creator and topic.
6.  Sign up and sign in.
7.  Follow creators.
8.  Save/bookmark work.
9.  Submit and publish a piece of work.
10. Select an origin disclosure label and provide optional process
    context.
11. Report content or a profile.

### MVP admin/editor features

-   Review new submissions before publication.
-   Feature or unfeature content.
-   Manage categories and tags.
-   Review user reports.
-   Suspend or remove content that violates policy.
-   View basic platform activity without collecting unnecessary personal
    information.

### Explicitly out of scope for the first MVP

-   Native mobile applications.
-   Direct messaging.
-   Livestreaming.
-   Complex recommendation machine learning.
-   Creator subscriptions and payouts.
-   NFT or blockchain features.
-   Automated AI-detection claims.
-   Full e-commerce and shipping.
-   Organisation dashboards.
-   A complicated verification programme.
-   Uploading every possible media format.

Start with a limited set of formats, such as images and text. Add audio
and video after upload, storage, moderation and playback are reliable.

## 10. Suggested technical architecture

### Recommended approach

Use a **modular monolith** for the first release. It is simpler to
build, deploy and maintain than microservices, while keeping boundaries
clear enough to split services later if necessary.

A practical stack:

  Layer                   Suggested technology    Purpose
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  Web application         Next.js + TypeScript    Public pages, creator
                                                  profiles and
                                                  authenticated
                                                  experiences

  UI                      Tailwind CSS v4         Responsive design and
                                                  visual system

  UI components           Accessible component    Consistent controls and
                          primitives              interactions

  Backend                 Next.js server          Business logic and data
                          routes/actions or a     access
                          separate Node.js API    

  Database                PostgreSQL              Users, creators,
                                                  content, follows,
                                                  saves, labels and
                                                  moderation

  ORM                     Prisma or Drizzle       Typed database access
                                                  and migrations

  Authentication          Auth.js or a reputable  Sign-in and sessions
                          managed auth provider   

  Media storage           S3-compatible object    Images and later
                          storage                 audio/video

  Image delivery          CDN and image           Fast media delivery
                          optimisation            

  Search                  PostgreSQL full-text    Basic content and
                          search initially        creator discovery

  Background work         Queue/worker when       Media processing,
                          needed                  notifications and
                                                  moderation tasks

  Testing                 Vitest and Playwright   Unit, integration and
                                                  end-to-end tests

  Deployment              A managed Next.js host  Reduce infrastructure
                          plus managed PostgreSQL work in the MVP
                          and object storage      
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

**Stack decision note:** The user is familiar with React, TypeScript,
Node.js, Express and MongoDB. If keeping a separate Express backend is
more comfortable, it is viable. PostgreSQL is recommended here because
the product has many connected entities and relationships (users,
creators, posts, categories, follows, saves, permissions and
moderation). Do not introduce both Express and Next.js APIs without a
clear reason; choose one primary application backend for the MVP.

### Core application modules

1.  **Identity** --- registration, login, sessions and account settings.
2.  **Creator profiles** --- biography, links, profile image and creator
    preferences.
3.  **Content publishing** --- drafts, submissions, publication status
    and edits.
4.  **Media** --- secure uploads, file validation, processing and
    delivery.
5.  **Discovery** --- categories, tags, search, curated collections and
    featured content.
6.  **Social actions** --- follows and bookmarks.
7.  **Origin and provenance** --- creator declarations, AI-use
    disclosure, evidence references and verification records.
8.  **Moderation** --- reports, review queues, policy actions and audit
    history.
9.  **Administration** --- editorial curation and platform
    configuration.

Keep these as modules in one codebase initially, not separate deployable
services.

## 12. Media and content pipeline

For media uploads:

1.  User selects a file.
2.  Server checks authentication, file size, allowed format and upload
    permission.
3.  Server issues a short-lived signed upload URL.
4.  Browser uploads directly to object storage.
5.  Server records the media asset and associates it with a draft.
6.  Background processing creates safe display derivatives and
    thumbnails.
7.  Content enters review or publication according to the platform's
    moderation policy.
8.  Public pages deliver optimised assets through a CDN.

Do not store large media files directly in PostgreSQL. Store object keys
and metadata in the database.

For the MVP, set clear upload limits and supported file types. Validate
file contents, not only file extensions. Consider malware scanning and
metadata stripping where appropriate. Preserve original files only when
there is a clear product reason and suitable privacy/security controls.

## 13. Discovery and ranking

Discovery is the heart of the product. Do not simply sort everything by
likes or posting frequency.

Start with a transparent, mostly editorial system:

-   Featured work selected by an editor/admin.
-   New and recently published work.
-   Category and topic pages.
-   Creator discovery.
-   Curated collections.
-   Simple relevance matching for search.

Later, ranking can consider: - Relevance to the user's selected
interests. - Content quality signals. - Diversity of creators and
categories. - Freshness without over-rewarding frequency. - Saves and
meaningful interactions. - Editorial selection. - User feedback such as
"show me less of this".

Avoid optimising only for time-on-platform, outrage, or raw engagement.
Do not let popularity permanently bury new creators.

## 14. Premium product and design direction

The product's premium feel should come from craft, not visual decoration
alone.

### Design characteristics

-   Strong typography and generous whitespace.
-   Excellent image presentation and cropping.
-   Calm, intentional layouts.
-   Clear hierarchy and restrained colour.
-   Fast loading and responsive interactions.
-   Consistent spacing, transitions and components.
-   High-quality creator and content detail pages.
-   Accessibility, keyboard navigation and readable contrast.
-   Minimal interruptions and no cluttered feed.

### Experience principles

-   Make the work the visual focus.
-   Give the creator a meaningful presence without overwhelming the
    creation.
-   Avoid aggressive notifications and engagement tricks.
-   Do not use infinite scroll as the only discovery mechanism.
-   Give users a sense of completion: collections, themed journeys and
    deliberate recommendations.
-   Make the platform feel alive through quality and freshness, not
    volume.

Do not attempt to imitate Apple's exact visual identity. Build a
recognisable design system of your own.

## 15. Trust, safety, rights and privacy

A platform hosting user-generated work needs clear policies from the
beginning.

### Creator rights

-   Creators retain ownership of their original work, subject to
    applicable law.
-   The platform receives only the limited licence necessary to host,
    display, distribute and promote the work as described in its terms.
-   Do not claim ownership of submitted content.
-   Explain whether public content can be indexed by search engines.
-   Provide a process for deleting content and accounts, subject to
    legal and operational retention requirements.
-   Provide a copyright complaint and dispute process.

Have the terms reviewed by a qualified lawyer before public launch,
especially if operating internationally.

### AI use

-   Ask creators to disclose material generative AI involvement.
-   Make labels understandable and consistent.
-   Do not treat AI detection as conclusive.
-   Allow creators to explain their process.
-   Publish the platform's own policy on whether submitted work may be
    used to train AI systems. A sensible starting position is not to
    grant AI-training rights by default.

### Privacy and security

-   Collect only information needed to operate the service.
-   Keep private evidence and moderation records access-controlled.
-   Use secure authentication and appropriate password/session
    protections.
-   Restrict administrative permissions.
-   Protect upload endpoints and validate file types and sizes.
-   Rate-limit sensitive endpoints.
-   Keep secrets out of source control.
-   Avoid logging private drafts or sensitive user content
    unnecessarily.
-   Provide reporting, blocking and moderation workflows as the
    community grows.

## 16. Business model possibilities

Do not finalise monetisation before validating the product and creator
community.

Potential options:

1.  **Freemium discovery:** Free browsing with optional premium
    collections or personal features.
2.  **Creator tools:** Paid advanced portfolios, analytics or
    presentation features.
3.  **Marketplace commissions:** A commission if creators sell work or
    experiences through the platform.
4.  **Membership:** A paid tier for enhanced discovery, curated
    experiences or community access.
5.  **Licensing and partnerships:** Carefully negotiated commercial use
    of creators' work, only with clear permission.
6.  **Brand collaborations:** Partnerships that respect creator control
    and platform values.

Avoid putting a paywall between the platform and all discovery before it
has built a meaningful audience. The initial priority is value, trust,
supply of excellent work and repeat discovery.

## 17. Validation before substantial development

The main risk is not whether the technology can be built. It is whether
enough creators will contribute and enough users will return to discover
their work.

Before building a large system:

1.  Interview 10--15 potential creators across photography, art,
    writing, music and crafts.
2.  Ask where they currently publish and what frustrates them about
    discovery.
3.  Ask what would make them publish on a new platform.
4.  Interview 10--15 potential audience members about how they find
    original work and what they dislike about current platforms.
5.  Create a simple landing page explaining the promise.
6.  Invite a small founding group of creators.
7.  Manually curate an initial collection.
8.  Test whether visitors explore multiple pieces, follow creators or
    return.

Do not ask only whether people "like the idea". Observe whether they
actually contribute, browse, save, follow and return.

## 18. Suggested development roadmap

### Phase 0 --- Product definition

-   Choose a temporary name.
-   Define the initial audience and content types.
-   Write the origin-label definitions.
-   Create a visual direction and clickable prototype.
-   Interview creators and potential users.

### Phase 1 --- Discovery experience

-   Build responsive public homepage.
-   Build content detail pages.
-   Build categories and curated collections.
-   Add creator profiles.
-   Seed the platform with permissioned sample content.

### Phase 2 --- Creator publishing

-   Add authentication.
-   Add creator onboarding.
-   Add drafts and publishing.
-   Add media uploads.
-   Add origin labels and optional process context.
-   Add submission review and moderation.

### Phase 3 --- Personal discovery

-   Add follows and bookmarks.
-   Add basic search and filters.
-   Add new and featured content.
-   Add user-selected interests.
-   Measure repeat visits and meaningful interactions.

### Phase 4 --- Trust and growth

-   Improve reporting and moderation.
-   Introduce proportionate evidence-based verification pilots.
-   Improve search and recommendations.
-   Build creator onboarding and community partnerships.
-   Explore monetisation only after repeat usage and creator value are
    demonstrated.

## 19. Success metrics

Do not measure success only by registrations, page views or time spent.

Early metrics: - Number of creators who complete onboarding. - Number of
creators who publish a first work. - Percentage of submissions that meet
quality and policy standards. - Number of meaningful discoveries per
visitor. - Content saves and creator follows. - Percentage of visitors
who return within 7 and 30 days. - Creator retention: creators who
publish again. - Audience retention: users who return to discover
more. - Qualitative feedback from creators and visitors.

A particularly important signal is whether creators feel their work is
presented and discovered better here than on the platforms they already
use.

## 21. Decisions still open

These should be explored rather than prematurely fixed:

-   Product name and brand identity.
-   Whether the initial focus is visual art, photography, writing or a
    broader mix.
-   Whether the first experience is open to everyone or
    curated/invitation-led.
-   How editorial curation and user recommendations should balance.
-   Exact definitions for human-created and AI-assisted.
-   Whether creators can sell work in the first major release.
-   Whether the product is free, freemium or membership-based.
-   Which countries and creator communities to target first.
-   How verification should work and what claims the platform can
    responsibly make.

## 22. The guiding idea

The product is not trying to stop AI from creating.

It is responding to a world in which AI-generated content is abundant by
making human creativity, perspective and experience easier to discover
and more rewarding to appreciate.

**The ambition is to make human-origin work feel valuable, visible and
worth seeking out.**

> **In a world where everything can be generated, experience something
> real.**
